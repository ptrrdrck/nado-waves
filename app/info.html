Nothing has ever measured a wave at these three breaks. This is physically derived, not an accurate forecast, and it carries no error bar because there is nothing to compute one against. What it claims is which break holds more of today's swell — not how big it is.

Each break's number is the buoy's swell carried by physics to where it breaks: through the windows the coast leaves open, bent over the seabed and slowed by its friction, diffracted round every window edge, lifted by shoaling into 5 m of water with local wind chop added, then carried in over the surveyed beach profile at the tide of the moment until it breaks. It is still not a wave height at the beach as a surfer would call it: a significant height, not a face height, and the step from one to the other has not been fitted; the beach profile is a 2016 survey rather than this season's sandbars; and nothing has checked it against the sand.

The charts under each “LIVE” card

The Swell card's charts are buoy 46232's own spectrum at every hour, carried in by the same chain as the reading above them. They open on the last day; zoom and pan them to go back to the start of the buoy's archive (4 August 2026). Every hour is rebuilt with today's windows, seabed and surf zone, so an earlier hour shows what the current chain makes of it, not what this page said at the time: the last week on every collection, the rest once a day. When the older hours no longer match today's chain they are not shown until they have been rebuilt, so two versions of the chain are never drawn on one line.

An hour with no buoy spectrum is a gap: a pale band with no line through it, at every zoom, never filled from the hour beside it. A break has no breaking height for an hour whose water level was not measured, which is every hour before 17 September 2026.

Each tab draws its own line only: Height is the break's breaking height, or the buoy's own on its tab. North vs. South is North's less South's; where it crosses zero, which of the two is bigger has flipped. Window is a break's height through its windows as a share of the buoy's, before the seabed; under it, a dot for each hour marks where the buoy's peak came from, over the break's open windows shaded. The share is the whole spectrum through the windows, so it moves with how widely the swell is spread as well as with where its peak sits. Range %K is where the buoy sits in its own last 24 hours' range, 0 at the low and 100 at the high, less where the break sits in its: a position in a range, not a height, left blank where too few of those 24 hours were measured.

Wind and Tide have charts of their own, on the same hours and the same window. The wind is the airfield's (KNZY) report taken in the hour up to each hour: its speed, a dot for any gust, and under it the direction it blows from, which a calm or a variable wind does not have. An hour with no report is a gap, never the report beside it. The tide is the San Diego gauge's sample at each hour exactly, carried out to the open coast as the card's is. Both are read straight from the collected records rather than rebuilt, so nothing done to the chain can change an earlier hour; they reach back a month, or to where the records begin (18 September 2026 for the wind, 17 September for the tide).

Past the present, the Tide chart carries the next 24 hours of harmonic prediction, dashed, with the gauge's measured departure from it over the last 3 days added: the one modelled line on this tab, as the card's next turn is its one modelled number, and labelled as one.

Each card has a second view in the same menu. Shore direction, on the Wind card, is how often the airfield's wind has been offshore, cross-shore or onshore at the break whose tab is open, one bar for each hour of the day on your clock. Each bar is that hour on every day counted, one reading a day, split by how many of those days it was each kind, with calm and variable as their own share: the same verdict the card prints, against that break's shore normal, counted over every hour this page holds. Departure, on the Tide card, is the gauge's measured level less its harmonic prediction at each hour, on the open coast, with the 3-day mean of it that the forecast's tide carries. An hour with no reading counts nowhere and is a gap.

The charts carry no description of their own; this page is where each is explained. Apart from the tide's dashed next day, nothing here is a forecast, and nothing here has been measured at the breaks.

Past hours: forecast against measurement

The Forecast tab reaches 48 hours back. For each hour that has gone by, the forecast figure is what this page showed for that hour: the newest run that had been published before it, which is not always the run on screen now. For older runs only the headline is kept, not the calculation behind it.

The red line under it is what buoy 46232 measured at that hour, carried in by the same chain the “LIVE” tab uses: the same windows, seabed and surf zone, with the airfield's wind and the gauge's water level from that hour. An hour with no buoy spectrum is shown as a gap. It is never filled from the hour beside it.

Because both figures go through the same physics, the difference between them is the model's error at the buoy, carried in. It does not check the beach. Nothing has been fitted from it, and nothing here has been measured at the breaks.

The charts under each Forecast card

The Swell card's are drawn from the model, every 3 hours, from 48 hours back to the end of the run. They open on three days with the present a quarter of the way in. A break's line is its breaking height only; an hour whose figure was at 5 m of water, or that no run covered, is a gap in the line, never the hour beside it.

Forecast + Observed is the newest GFS-Wave run's line over what each of the last eight runs said for the same hours, as the page showed them, and, beside the hours that have passed, buoy 46232 carried in by the same chain in red. Where the run lines bunch, the runs agree; where they fan out, the model has been changing its mind. Runs agreeing is not the same as being right, and the spread between them is not a range the swell will fall in. Swell trains is each train the chain carries in, as a dot at its period, larger for a bigger train, with the heading it comes from under it; a new swell arrives long-period first, so it shows as dots stepping down in period. The trains are split hour by hour, and a dot is not the same train as the one beside it. Trains are kept only for the runs whose hours were kept in full. North vs. South is North's forecast breaking height less South's.

Ensemble, on the buoy's tab only, is GEFS-Wave: the same wave model run 31 times from slightly different weather. The dashed line is the mean of its members at the buoy and the shading one standard deviation of them either side, beside the newest GFS-Wave run and the red line. It is total height at the buoy with no direction, so it cannot be carried through a break's windows and says nothing about the beach. The chart states how often buoy 46232 has fallen inside the shading, per lead time, and over which dates: measured so far, far less often than a range that size would imply, because the members agree with each other more closely than any of them agrees with the sea, and their mean runs above the buoy. Nothing has been removed from it or added to it to make it look better.

The Wind and Tide cards' charts are hourly, over the same reach: the National Weather Service's forecast wind at Coronado (the card's Local wind), with a dot for any gust and its direction under it, and the harmonic tide at the open coast with the gauge's measured departure added, as the card's is. Beside the hours that have passed, the airfield's measured wind and the gauge's measured level, in red. In grey, GFS-Wave's own wind, the one on the card: it is at the buoy, 29 km offshore, the wind the model makes its wind sea from, hourly to five days and every third hour after, joined across the hours it does not give. Shore direction here counts the local forecast's hours to the end of the run the same way, and Departure is the same measured series as on “LIVE”, in red.

Only the Ensemble view carries a band, and only beside how often the buoy it is drawn for has fallen inside it. No chart shows a difference between the forecast and the red line, and nothing here has measured a wave at the breaks.
